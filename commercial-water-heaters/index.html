Warning Signs to Watch For

Water heaters usually give plenty of warning before they fail, as long as you know what to look and listen for. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Visible corrosion or water pooling near the tank
  • Rumbling, popping, or banging from a heavily used tank
  • Dish or hand sink temperatures drifting below what your inspector expects
  • Unsteady delivery temperatures that suggest a failing mixing valve
  • Equipment approaching the end of its expected service life
  • Lukewarm water at the exact time you need the most
  • Distant fixtures that take minutes to run warm
  • Higher utility bills that point to a heater losing efficiency

The Benefits of Professional Commercial Water Heaters

The scale of commercial equipment changes the job. Gas input is often several times that of a home unit, which affects gas line sizing, meter capacity, combustion air, and venting. Electric models can draw enough current to require substantial service capacity. Storage often runs at 140 degrees or higher, so thermostatic mixing valves are needed to deliver safe water at fixtures, and those valves must be matched to the flow and tested. Recirculation loops need the right pump, working check valves, and balancing, or hot water short-circuits back to the heater and distant fixtures stay cold.

Code compliance and scheduling matter just as much. Permits and inspections are standard for commercial work, larger tanks may carry extra requirements, and any delay at inspection can keep a kitchen or laundromat closed. Murray Water Heater handles that coordination up front, plans installations for early mornings, evenings, or closed days, and replaces banked units one at a time whenever the layout allows. Tips to Keep Your Water Heater Healthy

Most of the failures we see could have been delayed for years with basic upkeep. Put these technician-approved steps to work at home:

  • Schedule upgrades during slow periods instead of waiting for a breakdown
  • Keep recirculation pumps and check valves in service to avoid long waits and wasted energy
  • Test thermostatic mixing valves periodically to confirm safe delivery temperatures
  • Set a flushing schedule based on usage; busy tanks collect sediment fast
  • Track temperatures at the dish machine and hand sinks as part of routine checks
  • Replace consumed anode rods before the tank itself starts to corrode
  • Do not store supplies against the equipment or block air openings
