A little caution before we arrive can prevent a much bigger mess. For a leak, close the cold supply valve at the top of the tank, then switch off the breaker for an electric model or set the gas control to "off" on a gas model. Move boxes and anything valuable away from the tank and put down towels to keep the water contained. Gas smells are never a wait-and-see situation: leave and call the gas utility from a safe spot.

Keeping Your West Jordan Water Heater Running Longer

The best way to avoid a surprise leak is simple maintenance. We recommend that homeowners flush the tank once a year (more often with hard water), have the anode rod checked every three to five years, test the relief valve once a year to make sure it isn't stuck, and keep the thermostat around 120 degrees, which limits scalding risk and slows mineral buildup. Tankless owners should plan on a descaling flush every year if their water is hard.

Equipment age plays a big role. With regular maintenance, a standard tank usually runs 8 to 12 years, a heat pump water heater 10 to 15, and a tankless unit up to 20. Once a heater passes that range and starts acting up, like rust-colored water, rumbling, or a damp spot under the tank, it's smart to start planning a replacement. Replacing on your own schedule is cheaper and is far less stressful than an emergency swap after a leak.

If warning signs do show up, acting quickly keeps them small. A dripping relief valve might point to high water pressure or thermal expansion with nowhere to go, both modest fixes. The same drip ignored for months can wear out the valve and stress the tank until the tank itself fails, and a small repair becomes a full replacement plus water damage. It's smart to keep our number saved on your phone so you can reach us at the first sign of trouble. Early intervention saves time, money, and ruined floors.

Hot Water Headaches West Jordan Homeowners Face

Every type of water heater fails in its own way, and we work on all of them around West Jordan. Gas tank heaters tend to have trouble with pilot assemblies and thermocouples, dirty burners, and venting that doesn't draft. Electric units lose heating elements and thermostats, especially when sediment piles up around the lower element. Tankless units throw error codes for scale in the heat exchanger, ignition failures, and flow sensor problems, most of which trace back to skipped descaling. Heat pump water heaters need clean air filters and a clear condensate drain, and they struggle in cramped closets. We service all four types regularly and stock the common parts to finish most repairs in one trip.
