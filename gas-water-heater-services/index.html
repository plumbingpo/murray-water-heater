A gas water heater is a refreshingly simple machine, which is part of why it lasts. A burner under the tank heats the water, exhaust rises through a flue that runs up the center of the tank, and a gas control valve on the front acts as both thermostat and safety shutoff. That valve only lets gas reach the burner while it senses a flame, through a thermocouple on standing-pilot models or a flame sensor on electronic ignition models. Most failures trace back to one of those parts. In Murray, UT, we regularly find thermocouples that no longer hold the valve open, pilots starved by a lint-clogged flame arrestor screen, gas valves that have failed internally, and flue or vent problems that let exhaust spill back into the room.

Diagnosing a gas water heater properly means checking combustion, not just parts. Across Murray, our technicians measure thermocouple output in millivolts, study the pilot and burner flame for color and shape, read gas pressure at the valve, and test draft at the hood so we know exhaust is leaving the house. We check the combustion chamber for soot and scorching, which point to a blocked flue or poor combustion air, and we test for carbon monoxide near the heater. Keep one rule in mind: a gas smell means leaving the house and calling the gas utility from outside before anything else, and Murray Water Heater can take over from there.

Once we know the cause, the repair is usually simple. A new thermocouple or flame sensor, a cleaned pilot tube and burner, a cleared flame arrestor screen, or a replacement gas control valve gets most heaters running again the same day. Vent problems, like a disconnected connector or a flue pipe without enough rise, are corrected on the spot. If sediment is rumbling in the tank, we flush it, and if the anode rod is spent, we can replace it. When replacement is the smarter move, we install new gas heaters sized to your household's first-hour demand, with proper atmospheric, power vent, or direct vent venting, a drip leg on the gas supply, and the expansion tank, drain pan, and strapping your local code calls for. When to Call for Gas Water Heater Services

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you money and keep a slow drip from turning into a flooded utility room. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Loud rumbling from the bottom of the tank
  • A yellow, lazy, or sooty burner flame instead of a crisp blue one
  • Pilot outages that keep coming back after a relight
  • A cold tank and a burner that will not come on
  • Leaks at the drain valve, the fittings, or the bottom of the tank
  • Any smell of gas around the appliance, which calls for leaving and phoning the gas utility
  • Showers that go cold sooner than they did a year ago
  • Exhaust odor, rust, or moisture around the draft hood and vent pipe

A Slow Leak Never Stays Slow

Left alone, even minor symptoms like rumbling or temperatures that swing from hot to warm tend to mean the heater is working its way toward a breakdown. If you notice any of these signs, contact Murray Water Heater at (801) 998-0110 for a prompt inspection.

Why Hire a Professional for Gas Water Heater Services?

Two things make gas water heater work a poor DIY project: the parts involve fuel and combustion, and the symptoms overlap. A pilot outage might come from a worn thermocouple, a partly blocked pilot tube, lint on the flame arrestor screen, a downdraft, or an internal valve failure. Water Heater Care Tips From Our Technicians

A little routine care goes a long way toward keeping a water heater running well past its expected lifespan. Here are our recommendations:

  • Watch for a loose, rusted, or sagging vent pipe above the heater
  • Replace a spent anode rod before the tank itself starts to rust
  • Flush a few gallons from the drain valve periodically to control sediment
  • Install carbon monoxide alarms on every level of the home and test them monthly
  • Choose a setting of about 120 degrees to balance comfort, safety, and efficiency
  • Keep the area around the heater clear, especially of gasoline, paint, and solvents
  • Clean dust from around the base so the burner gets plenty of combustion air
