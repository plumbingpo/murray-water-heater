Warning Signs to Watch For

A heater that is starting to struggle rarely hides it for long, and the clues are easy to spot once you know them. These are the red flags our technicians see most often before a breakdown:

  • Signs of scorching or backdrafting around the base or top of a gas heater
  • Sudden loud knocking or boiling sounds coming from inside the tank
  • Tap water hot enough to burn, even at the usual setting
  • A T&P valve that releases water repeatedly instead of staying closed
  • Water pooling around the base of the heater or spreading across the floor
  • An electric water heater that keeps tripping its circuit breaker
  • No hot water at all, even after waiting for the tank to recover
  • A rotten-egg smell near a gas water heater (leave first, and call the gas utility)

The Benefits of Professional Emergency Water Heater Repair

A water heater emergency stacks several hazards in one small space. There is water on the floor near a 240-volt circuit on electric models, or near a gas valve, burner, and flue on gas models. There is a tank holding water at high temperature and household pressure. And there is a safety valve that, if it is discharging, is doing so for a reason. Homeowners sometimes try to stop the relief valve by capping it, tighten a leaking fitting until it cracks, or relight a pilot beside a gas smell, and each of those can turn a bad afternoon into a real disaster. The safest moves for a homeowner are the ones described above: leave for gas, then shut off water and energy, then call for help.

A trained technician brings the right order of operations and a truck full of the likely parts. Our team confirms the heater is isolated, tests for gas leaks with a detector and soap solution rather than guessing, checks household water pressure and thermal expansion when a relief valve has been discharging, and inspects the vent for proper draft before a gas unit is restarted. Equally important, we tell you honestly when a heater can be repaired and when it cannot. A leaking seam or rusted-through bottom cannot be fixed, while a failed valve, fitting, or element usually can, and the difference is often a modest repair bill versus the cost of a whole new unit. Tips to Keep Your Water Heater Healthy

Most of the failures we see could have been delayed for years with basic upkeep. Here are our recommendations:

  • Test the T&P valve annually; a valve that sticks or weeps afterward should be replaced
  • Annual flushing keeps sediment from causing the rumbling and hot spots that lead to leaks
  • Know where the heater shutoff and the main water valve are, and make sure everyone in the house does too
  • Store chemicals and flammables far from the burner, and keep the area around the heater uncluttered
  • Ask about a drain pan and drain line, especially for heaters in attics, closets, or above finished rooms
  • Consider an automatic leak shutoff that stops the cold supply the moment water hits the floor
  • Set the temperature around 120F, which protects against burns and slows tank wear
