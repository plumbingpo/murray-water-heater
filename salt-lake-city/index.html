Getting More Years From Your Water Heater in Salt Lake City

Most early failures start with maintenance that never happened, and the habits that prevent them are simple. Flush the tank once a year to clear sediment, have the anode rod inspected every few years, and operate the relief valve annually so it doesn't seize. Keep the temperature near 120 degrees to reduce scald risk and slow scale buildup. For a tankless heater in a hard-water area, plan on descaling once a year to protect the heat exchanger.

Take a look at the age of your water heater. The serial number on the label usually includes the manufacture date, and its age tells you a lot. Most tank models make it to 8 to 12 years, heat pump units 10 to 15, and well-maintained tankless units about 20. If your heater is beyond that point and also shows rusty water, popping sounds, or dampness under the tank, it's a strong candidate for a planned replacement, which is almost always cheaper and easier on everyone than a rushed emergency install once the tank fails.

The sooner you call about a hot water problem, the smaller the repair tends to be. A tank that's just begun to rumble can often be fixed with a flush, yet the same sediment, ignored for a few years, can overheat the bottom of the tank and burn out the lower element on an electric model, forcing a replacement much sooner. Save our number in your contacts today so help is one call away the moment something seems off. A quick call saves time, money, and ruined floors.
