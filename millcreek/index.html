How Millcreek Homeowners Can Extend Water Heater Life

Most premature tank failures trace back to maintenance that never happened, and the habits that prevent them are simple. Flush the tank once a year to clear sediment, get the anode rod checked every few years, and operate the relief valve each year so it doesn't seize. Keep the temperature near 120 degrees to reduce scald risk and slow scale buildup. If you have a tankless unit, descale it yearly where the water is hard to protect the heat exchanger.

How old is your water heater? The serial number on the label usually encodes the month and year it was built, and that date tells you a lot. Most tank models reach 8 to 12 years, heat pump models 10 to 15, and well-maintained tankless units around 20. If your heater is beyond that point and is also showing rusty water, rumbling, or dampness under the tank, it's usually due for a planned replacement, and that's almost always cheaper and less stressful than a rushed emergency install after a leak.

The sooner you call about a hot water problem, the smaller the repair tends to be. A tank that's just begun to rumble can often be fixed with a flush, yet that same buildup, ignored through a few more years, can overheat the bottom of the tank and burn out the lower element on an electric model, forcing a replacement much sooner. Save our number in your contacts now so you can reach us fast the moment something sounds or looks wrong. Early intervention saves time, money, and cold showers.
