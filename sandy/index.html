How Sandy Homeowners Can Extend Water Heater Life

The best way to avoid a surprise leak is routine maintenance. We recommend that homeowners flush the tank once a year (more often with hard water), have the anode rod checked every three to five years, test the relief valve once a year to make sure it isn't stuck, and keep the thermostat around 120 degrees, which limits scalding risk and cuts down on scale. Tankless owners should plan on a descaling flush every year in hard-water areas.

Take a look at the age of your water heater. The serial number on the label usually includes the month and year it was built, and that date tells you a lot. Most tank heaters make it to 8 to 12 years, heat pump units 10 to 15, and well-maintained tankless units about 20. If your heater is beyond that range and also shows discolored water, popping sounds, or moisture at the base, it's a strong candidate for a planned replacement, and that's almost always cheaper and less stressful than an emergency replacement after a leak.

The sooner you call about a hot water problem, the smaller the repair tends to be. A tank that's just begun to rumble needs only a flush, yet the same sediment, ignored through a few more years, can create hot spots on the tank floor and burn out the lower element on an electric model, turning a simple visit into a replacement. Save our number on your phone today so help is one call away as soon as something sounds or looks wrong. A quick call saves time, money, and cold showers.

Common Water Heater Problems in Sandy

Water heaters in and around Sandy deal with a predictable set of stresses. Depending on the neighborhood and the water source, hard water may be part of the picture: dissolved minerals drop out as sediment on the tank floor, forcing the burner or lower element to work harder, which produces the popping and rumbling you may hear when the heater fires. Tankless units aren't immune either: scale builds inside the heat exchanger, and that's why annual descaling matters so much in hard-water areas.

Age matters just as much. Plenty of homes around here still have a tank that was installed a decade or more ago, and once a tank is past about ten years, the anode rod has usually been consumed and rust starts working on the steel from the inside. Homes here also run on a mix of fuel types, gas tanks in some houses and electric tanks in others, which means we service both sides: pilot assemblies, gas valves, and flues on gas units, and elements, thermostats, and breakers on electric ones.

Homes on private wells can bring sediment, iron, or sulfur into the tank, and a sulfur smell in the hot water often means a reaction with the anode rod that a different rod can fix. Older installations often need code updates when a heater is replaced: a thermal expansion tank on closed systems, a correctly piped T&P discharge line, drain pans where a leak would hit finished floors, and earthquake straps in areas that require them. All of these can be fixed, and most are much cheaper to address early. If your water heater is doing anything unusual, a diagnostic visit from our team catches the small problem before it becomes an expensive one. Call (801) 998-0110 and we'll take a look.
