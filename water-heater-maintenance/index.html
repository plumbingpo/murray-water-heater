If you could see inside your water heater, the case for maintenance would make itself. On the bottom there is a bed of mineral sediment, sometimes several inches deep, that makes the burner work harder, creates popping and rumbling, and can burn out the lower element on an electric unit. Hanging from the top is the anode rod, meant to corrode first, which may be down to bare wire after a few years. When it is used up, corrosion goes after the tank lining, and a leak is only a matter of time. Across Murray, UT, a yearly visit is the simplest way to keep both of those problems from shortening the life of the heater.

Each visit follows the same thorough checklist. The tank is drained and flushed to clear the sediment layer. The anode rod comes out for inspection and is replaced when little of it remains. The T&P relief valve is tested so we know it will open under pressure and close again cleanly. Gas heaters get a look at the burner, pilot assembly, thermocouple or flame sensor, flue, and draft. Electric heaters get element and thermostat testing and a check of the wiring connections. We also measure the air charge in the expansion tank, check the water pressure, set the thermostat around 120 degrees unless a mixing valve allows higher storage, and inspect the drain valve, connections, and pan.

We maintain tankless units as well. In place of a sediment flush, they need periodic descaling, since hard water leaves mineral deposits in the heat exchanger that restrict flow and reduce efficiency. We isolate the unit, circulate a descaling solution through it, clean the inlet filter, check the vent and condensate line, and review stored error codes for patterns. Whatever type you have, you receive a plain summary of the unit's condition and any parts approaching the end of their life. For water heater maintenance in Murray, call (801) 998-0110.

Signs You Need Water Heater Maintenance in Murray

A heater that is starting to struggle rarely hides it for long, and the clues are easy to spot once you know them. These are the red flags our technicians see most often before a breakdown:

  • Less hot water available than when the tank was new
  • Water in the drain pan or on the floor near the heater
  • Rumbling, popping, or crackling from the tank as it heats
  • A tankless unit showing error codes or fluctuating temperatures
  • A smell like rotten eggs that shows up only on the hot side
  • No memory of the last time the water heater was serviced
  • Rising utility bills that point to lost efficiency
  • Brown or orange tint in the hot water only

How to Prevent Water Heater Problems

A little routine care goes a long way toward keeping a water heater running well past its expected lifespan. Here are our recommendations:

  • Glance at the pan and connections regularly so small leaks are caught early
  • Treat hard water to cut down on sediment and scale
  • Replace a heavily consumed anode rod to extend the tank's life
  • Plan yearly descaling for a tankless heater, sooner if scale warnings appear
  • Keep at least a couple of feet clear around the heater for air and service access
  • Schedule a flush every year, or every six months if you have hard water
  • Avoid turning the tank up past 120 degrees unless a mixing valve is installed
